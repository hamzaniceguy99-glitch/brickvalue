How we work

A trader that puts the renovation bill on the table

Brickvalue is a company incorporated under Belgian law, registered with the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises under enterprise number 0748.467.044, based at Wezelsebaan in 2900 Schoten and trading since June 2020. Its activity is the purchase and sale of real estate on its own account: residential and non-residential buildings and land.

Two Flemish obligations determine what a purchase actually costs. A building constructed before 2001 requires an asbestos certificate before it can be transferred, which sets out what asbestos-containing material is present and in what condition. And since 2023, a buyer acquiring a dwelling with an energy performance label of E or F must renovate it to at least label D within five years of the deed. That second obligation binds the purchaser rather than the seller, which is precisely why a seller has no natural incentive to mention it — and why we state the label and whether the obligation applies before any offer is made. We also disclose the planning position, provide the soil and electrical documents, and say plainly when a report is unfavourable. We sell our own property and we are not an estate agent: that profession is regulated and requires a registration we neither hold nor claim.

Frequently asked

The questions we get most

What is the renovation obligation and does it apply to me?

In the Flemish Region, a buyer acquiring a dwelling with an energy label of E or F must renovate it to reach at least label D within five years of the authentic deed. It binds you as the purchaser, not the seller. That matters commercially because the work can cost a great deal more than the discount that made the property look attractive. We tell you the label and whether the obligation applies before you make an offer.

Why would a seller not mention that?

Because the obligation is the buyer's, so it costs the seller nothing to leave unsaid, and a property at label F looks cheap until you price the route to label D. We would rather you factored it in and offered accordingly than discovered it afterwards and felt misled. It is a question worth putting to any seller in Flanders, not only to us.

What does the asbestos certificate tell me?

Which asbestos-containing materials are present in a pre-2001 building, their condition, and how they should be managed. It is required before such a building can be transferred in Flanders. It is genuinely useful rather than a formality: material in good condition that can be left alone is a very different proposition from material that needs removal by a licensed firm.

Are you an estate agent?

No. We buy and sell property we own ourselves. The profession of estate agent is regulated in Belgium and requires registration with the professional institute, which we do not hold and do not claim — we do not act for third parties and we hold no third-party money. You are dealing with a principal whose interests are not yours, which is exactly why your notary should be your adviser rather than us.

The electrical report is unfavourable. Is that a dealbreaker?

Not necessarily, and you need to know before you commit. An unfavourable report means the installation must be brought into compliance, with a cost and a deadline that belong in the negotiation. What would be unacceptable is letting you find it in an annexe after signing, which is how these documents are too often handled.

Can you tell me whether this is a good investment?

No, and we are the wrong party to ask. We are the seller, and we provide no investment, tax or legal advice. We will give you the documents, the label, the obligations and the planning position accurately. What that is worth to you is a judgement for you and your own advisers to make.
